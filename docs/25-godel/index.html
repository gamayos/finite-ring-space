Incompleteness Without Infinity

\chgtext{Gödel’s incompleteness theorems are widely read as evidence against finitism. This paper examines the phenomenon under a finite ontology and argues that the reading is mistaken. Over a fixed finite structure the hypotheses of the theorems fail, and the structure’s theory is complete and decidable; the phenomenon then decomposes rather than vanishes.

B1p25005The setting: a structure $\M$ in a finite signature $\sigma$ with $\lvert\M\rvert=m\ge2$ finite. $L$ is its first-order language, and $\varphi\in\Th(\M)$ abbreviates $\M\models\varphi$, likewise $\ThSO(\M)$ for second-order sentences under full semantics. The running example is $\M_p=(\{0,\dots,p-1\};0,1,+,\times)$, arithmetic modulo a prime. Rows E1, F7, F8, G5 and G8 are stated over $\M_p$.D

B2p25006Iteration and induction: iteration names the successor act, total on a finite cycle, where it wraps. “Unbounded iteration” abbreviates the freshness claim, a successor injective and never returning to zero. Induction names the proof principle. The two are independent. A finite ontology keeps induction and the act, and declines the freshness claim.D

B3p25007The agent: capacity $(K,H)$ over an alphabet of $a\ge2$ letters, at most $K$ symbols of storage and $H$ elementary steps. A certificate is a retained, step-checked record of a $\D$-derivation with one designated end sentence, of length at most $B=\min(K,H)$.D

B4p25008Local soundness: a rule is locally sound if it preserves truth in every structure that agrees with $\M$ on the table entries its check consults. An axiom rule is locally sound when its sentence is true in every such structure. An axiom asserted on syntactic recognition consults nothing.D

E1p25019Abundance: the closed terms over $\{1,+\}$ with $\ell$ leaves are the full binary trees, $C_{\ell-1}\ge2^{\ell-2}$ in number. In $\M_p$ all have the value $\ell\bmod p$, so any two give a true equation of length $O(\ell)$, at least $2^{\ell}$ of them for $\ell\ge6$. E2p25020Vanishing reach: an agent of capacity $(K,H)$ certifies at most $a^{B+1}$ sentences over all its runs, the records being strings of length at most $B$. H1p25035The effectivity clause: among consistent, effectively axiomatised theories, interpreting $\mathsf{Q}$ forces incompleteness. $\Th(\N)$ is complete at grade (i) only, presentable by nothing.
